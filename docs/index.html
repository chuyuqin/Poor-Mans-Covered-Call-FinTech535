Poor man’s covered call
AMZN research study

I tested whether replacing 100 shares with a long-dated call reduces the cash needed for a covered-call strategy without materially weakening the result. The PMCC required less cash initially, but it did not outperform the conventional covered call.

Assessment. PMCC is a workable substitute when reducing the initial cash commitment is the objective. This test does not support choosing it for higher returns: most of its profit came from the long calls, while selling short calls added only $635.20 after commissions. I would require a broader sample and broker-specific assignment checks before deploying it.

01Study design

The choices below define a manageable single-stock case study. They were not selected by comparing strategy returns across stocks or searching for the best parameter combination.

DecisionReasonWhat it leaves unresolved
AMZNListed calls, no cash dividends, and a test window after its June 2022 split simplify the stock-replacement comparison.One surviving growth stock; no evidence of performance across other names.
2023–2025 · three full yearsA completed calendar-year sample permits repeated monthly trades and long-call replacements; it excludes partial-year 2026.A strong rising-stock sample. Starting in 2023 omits the preceding decline; the window is not a full market-cycle test.
0.80 long / 0.25 short deltaThe long provides substantial stock exposure; the short leaves room for a rise before its strike is reached.Judgment-based targets, not estimated optima. Delta does not eliminate loss or assignment risk.
One contract · $50,000 per accountEqual starting cash and one 100-share contract unit make the cash flows directly comparable without borrowing.Different delta and invested capital. Account returns depend on the deliberately idle cash balance.
Monthly calls · fixed strike gridA bounded, reproducible expired-contract universe keeps the historical download and selection process tractable.Weeklies and intermediate strikes are omitted; this is not the full historical chain.

The memo explains the maturity ranges, roll dates and execution filters. These choices are practical assumptions; the cost and assignment scenarios do not validate the parameter choices.

03Trading rules

Build a one-to-one call diagonal

1

Buy one in-the-money call: 0.75–0.85 delta, 365–540 days to expiration. Target 0.80 delta.

2

Sell one out-of-the-money call: 0.20–0.30 delta, 30–45 days to expiration. Target 0.25 delta; its strike must exceed the long strike.

3

Close the short after the prior session reaches 7 days remaining. Replace the long after it reaches 180 days, closing the short first.

Signal and execution timing

t

Rank contracts using the previous session’s delta, quotes, volume and remaining maturity.

t+1

Use the selected contracts at the next session’s ask to buy and bid to sell. Reject failed entry checks; do not replace the contract using future Greeks.

Entries require positive two-sided quotes, spread ≤ $2 and ≤ 20% of midpoint, and at least one signal-day trade. New-pair debit plus fees must be below strike width × 100. Long purchase must be funded before short proceeds arrive.

The first simulated entry · 2023-01-04: buy the $70 call expiring 2024-06-21 for $28.10 per share; sell the $100 call expiring 2023-02-17 for $1.45. Net cash paid including both fees: $2,666.30. The long leg alone costs $2,810.65; 100 shares at that day’s ask cost $8,515.00.

The first PMCC entry occurs one session after the stock benchmark starts. The capital comparison above uses the same date. Standard 100-share contracts are assumed; AMZN had no stock split or cash dividend during the test period. This is a cash-funded model, not a broker-specific margin calculation.

The early-assignment scenario forces assignment when an in-the-money short call’s bid is within $0.05 of intrinsic value at the prior close. Shares are delivered at strike; a PMCC short-stock position is bought back at the next session’s ask. The long call is retained. This tests an adverse event path, not its probability.

Baseline early assignment is not modeled. Scheduled pre-expiry closes produced zero expiration assignments. AMZN paid no dividends, but American-call early exercise is still possible. Daily data do not establish synchronized or fresh executable quotes, depth, or atomic multi-leg fills.

07Interpretation

Read the research memo ↗

Lower initial outlay; no return advantage.

The PMCC produced $14,470.00 of net profit on a $50,000 account. The initial long-call purchase required less cash than 100 shares. The covered-call account finished $1,118.20 ahead. I would treat the cash saving as a funding choice, not evidence of higher expected returns.

The long call adds expiration, time-decay and volatility exposure. The short call gives up part of a rally. AMZN’s strong rise over this sample makes this a favorable setting for bullish exposure; one stock and three years cannot establish robustness across market regimes.
